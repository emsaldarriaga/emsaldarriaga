Interventions create value only when they reflect the priorities and constraints of the people expected to use, deliver, or fund them. I use preference-elicitation methods, including discrete-choice experiments, to examine how patients, providers, payers, and other stakeholders weigh benefits, risks, costs, convenience, and access. By identifying preference heterogeneity and potential barriers to adoption, this research informs program design, implementation strategies, and decisions about which solutions are most likely to be accepted and sustained.
